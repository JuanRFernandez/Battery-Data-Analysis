# Case Brief — Energy Data Analyst

> An agnostic restatement of the original challenge brief. The source data covers the German–Luxembourg (DE-LU) day-ahead market for the first six months of 2022.

## Introduction

The goal is to bridge business needs and data-driven insights — turning raw market and weather data
into actionable conclusions for flexibility trading. The two tasks below mirror the kind of
questions a senior energy data analyst faces day to day.

**Format.** Provide the code and analysis in a Python Jupyter notebook with enough documentation to
follow the logic, plus the visualisations used to interpret the data and reach conclusions.

**Constraints.** Data availability is limited on some topics — taking shortcuts and making
assumptions is fine, as long as they are stated.

**Background — arbitrage / wholesale trading revenue.** Revenue generated by buying and selling
electricity: ideally, energy is stored when prices are low and sold when prices are high.

## Task 1 — The Battery Business Case

Two datasets contain electricity prices for the first six months of 2022:

- **15-minute contracts** (`data/1_Day-ahead_Prices_15min.csv`) — delivery of electricity at a
  constant power over 15 minutes.
- **60-minute contracts** (`data/1_Day-ahead_Prices_60min.csv`) — the same for 60-minute contracts.

Summarise and compare both datasets statistically. Which patterns do they share, and which differ?
Considering a battery traded to generate arbitrage / wholesale revenue, recommend which contract
type is more beneficial to trade. Document the logic behind the code and present the key insights and
visualisations in whatever format works best.

## Task 2 — Data Exploration (Weather & PV)

Two datasets are provided for the same time periods:

- **PV production** (`data/2_pv_production_pool.csv`) — time-series PV production from a *pool* (a
  group of households producing energy via PV panels within the same geographic region).
- **Weather** (`data/2_weather.csv`) — weather indicators (temperature, cloud cover, solar
  radiation, etc.).

Analyse how different weather variables influence PV production. Provide a statistical summary and
examine daily and seasonal patterns. Identify potential anomalies or unexpected behaviour, and
explore how weather patterns could improve PV production forecasts for a VPP (virtual power plant) by
conceptualising a forecast model based on the key predictive variables (building the model is not
mandatory).

### Optional subtask

How do you think the observed PV production impacts market prices?

## Weather field descriptions

| Field | Description |
| --- | --- |
| `forecast_datetime` | Timestamp of the predicted weather; start of the 1-hour forecast period. |
| `temperature` | Air temperature, in degrees Celsius. |
| `dewpoint` | Dew point temperature. |
| `cloudcover_[low/mid/high/total]` | Percentage of sky covered by clouds in altitude bands 0–2 km, 2–6 km, 6+ km, and total. |
| `10_metre_[u/v]_wind_component` | The eastward (`u`) / northward (`v`) component of wind speed measured at 10 m. |
| `direct_solar_radiation` | Direct solar radiation reaching a plane perpendicular to the Sun, accumulated over the hour, in Wh/m². |
| `surface_solar_radiation_downwards` | Direct + diffuse solar radiation reaching a horizontal plane at the surface, accumulated over the hour, in Wh/m². |
| `snowfall` | Snowfall over an hour, in metres of water equivalent. |
| `total_precipitation` | Accumulated liquid (rain + snow) reaching the surface over the hour, in metres. |
